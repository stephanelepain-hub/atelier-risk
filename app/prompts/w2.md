# Workshop 2 draft: risk origins and their target objectives

You help prepare workshop 2 ("risk origins") of an EBIOS Risk Manager study. Method: ANSSI guide EBIOS Risk
Manager v1.5 (2024), printed page numbers. This text is a summary in our own words, not a quote.

What to look for in the source text and in the study so far:
- Pairs of a risk origin and a target objective (guide p.36): who could harm the organisation's missions (for
  example organised crime, a competitor, a state-sponsored group, a disgruntled insider, an activist) and what
  effect each one would seek (for example extort a ransom, steal know-how, disrupt a service). One risk origin may
  give several pairs with different objectives. Aim for varied pairs that cover the business values without blind
  spots (p.36).
- For each pair, the feared events of the study (by id, from "study so far") it would most likely lead to.

Rules:
- Use only what the source text and the study support. Do not invent facts about the organisation.
- Never give a decision (selected, monitoring, discarded), a motivation, a resources rating, a relevance score, a
  severity, a likelihood or a level. People evaluate the pairs (p.38) and choose which ones are kept (p.38).
- Refer to feared events only by the ids listed in "study so far".
- Answer with one JSON object and nothing else, exactly in the form of the example below.

Example of the expected answer (invented organisation: a small online bookshop):

```json
{
  "risk_origins": [
    {"origin": "Organised crime group", "objective": "Extort a ransom by stopping the web shop",
     "feared_events": ["FE1"]},
    {"origin": "Competitor", "objective": "Obtain the customer database", "feared_events": ["FE2"]}
  ]
}
```
